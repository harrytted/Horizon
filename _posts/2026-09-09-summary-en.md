## [E-Ink Display Becomes a Networked Printer via Virtual IPP](https://nishantjosh.dev/blogs/how-to-build-a-fking-printer/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The article describes how to turn an e-ink display into a networked 'printer' by implementing a virtual IPP (Internet Printing Protocol) server. When a user 'prints' a document from any application, it is rendered directly on the screen instead of being sent to paper. This creative hardware hack shows that standard printing protocols can drive non-paper output devices, broadening what e-ink screens can do. Because IPP is the standard modern network printing protocol, any OS print dialog can send to the device, making the e-ink panel a practical document receiver. Implementation details include advertising the display's exact dimensions as supported media sizes (via media-size-supported and IPP names such as om_NNNmmxYYYmm), so the source computer formats documents precisely for the screen without needing device-side scaling. The author also declares media types like 'stationery' and an output bin such as 'face-up' to make the virtual printer behave like a standard, well-defined printer.

hackernews · cat-whisperer · Sep 8, 21:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49617255)

**Background**: IPP is a client-server protocol used for network printing that lets clients query printer capabilities, submit print jobs, and check job status; it has largely replaced older protocols such as LPD and port 9100. A virtual printer is software that presents the same interface as a printer driver but processes output in a different way, such as saving to PDF or sending it to another device. E-ink displays are low-power, reflective screens commonly used in e-readers. By combining these ideas, the article demonstrates how an e-reader's screen can behave like a network-attached paperless printer.

## [Terence Tao warns AI mining open math problems could exhaust the ecosystem](https://mathstodon.xyz/@tao/117237320796901560) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Terence Tao, a Fields medalist, warned on Mathstodon that AI systems indiscriminately solving open mathematical problems may be 'non-renewably mining' a finite ecosystem. He argues that this prioritizes short-term answers over the long-term health of mathematics. Tao's warning challenges the assumption that more machine-generated proofs are an unalloyed good. It frames AI's impact on mathematics as an ecology question: if important problems are consumed without yielding insight, future generations of mathematicians may lose the stepping-stones needed for deep progress. In the original post, Tao reportedly writes that 'the identification of a promising problem is now the scarce and precious resource,' shifting the bottleneck from solving problems to asking the right questions. He describes indiscriminate solution-finding as resource extraction that can achieve immediate goals while undermining the ecosystem for future waves of progress.

hackernews · _alternator_ · Sep 8, 21:00 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49616968)

**Background**: Open mathematical problems are valuable not only because their solutions settle a question, but because solutions often introduce new concepts, methods, and connections that open further avenues of research. In the mathematical community, an ecosystem of open problems functions as a source of incremental progress; the insights gained while solving one problem frequently generate the tools for the next. If AI systems can produce verified answers without human-understandable explanations, that generative process may be lost or curtailed. Tao's post speaks to both mathematicians and AI researchers about how to treat that ecosystem responsibly.

**Discussion**: Commenters in the Hacker News discussion are divided: some, like dvt, question whether a solution without insight truly blocks progress, since most interesting problems carry no prize and scoops rarely matter. Others agree with Tao and cite Asimov's 'Jokester' to argue that the scarce resource is now asking meaningful questions, not answering them, with the next frontier being AI that identifies promising open problems.
